The Smallest Sphere

A cosmic milestone

Hydrostatic Equilibrium

  • Mimas is the smallest known astronomical body in the solar system that is rounded in shape because of its own gravity.
  • Bodies smaller than Mimas don't have enough mass/gravity to pull themselves into a sphere, so they look like lumpy potatoes!
💥

Herschel Crater

The near-fatal blow

A Massive Impact

  • Mimas is famous for a giant crater named Herschel, which is 130 km across.
  • That crater spans almost one-third of the entire moon's diameter!
  • The impact was so massive that shockwaves travelled right through the moon, creating giant fractures on the exact opposite side. A slightly larger impact would have shattered it completely.
🛰️

The Death Star

A cinematic coincidence

Life Imitates Art

  • Because of the massive Herschel Crater, Mimas looks exactly like the Death Star from the movie Star Wars.
  • Here's the crazy part: Star Wars came out in 1977. The first clear photos of Mimas showing the crater weren't taken until Voyager 1 arrived in 1980! It was a complete coincidence.
🧹

Ring Sweeper

The Cassini Division

Orbital Resonance

  • If you look at Saturn's rings, there is a massive dark gap between the A and B rings called the Cassini Division.
  • Mimas is responsible for this gap! Its gravity pulls repeatedly on any ice particles in that region (a 2:1 resonance), literally sweeping the gap clean of debris.
🌊

A Stealth Ocean!

The massive 2024 Discovery

Hiding in Plain Sight

The Mystery Wobble: Scientists studying data from the Cassini mission noticed Mimas wobbles slightly in its orbit (called libration).
Liquid Water: In February 2024, astronomers confirmed this wobble is caused by a global liquid water ocean hiding 20-30 km beneath the icy crust! It's a brand new ocean world!

The Pac-Man Map

A weird temperature anomaly

More Gaming Coincidences

  • When the Cassini probe mapped the surface temperatures of Mimas, scientists found something hilarious.
  • The heat distribution pattern created a shape that looks exactly like Pac-Man eating the Herschel crater!
  • This happens because different types of ice on the surface absorb and release heat at different rates.
🧊

Composition

What is it made of?

A Dirty Snowball

  • Mimas has a very low density (1.15 g/cm³), meaning it is composed almost entirely of water ice with only a small amount of rock.
  • Because it lacks internal heat escaping to the surface (unlike Enceladus), its surface remains heavily cratered and ancient, hiding the ocean deep below.
🔭

Discovery

Who found it?

William Herschel

  • Discovered in 1789 by astronomer William Herschel (who the giant crater is named after).
  • It was studied up close by Voyager 1 and 2 in the 1980s, and extensively mapped by the Cassini spacecraft between 2004 and 2017.
