The Giant Moon

How big is it?

Bigger Than a Planet

  • It is the largest moon of Saturn, and the 2nd largest moon in the entire solar system (behind Ganymede).
  • It is physically larger than the planet Mercury, though Mercury is heavier due to its iron core.
☁️

The Atmosphere

Why is it so hazy?

A Thick Blanket

  • Titan is the only moon in the solar system with a dense atmosphere.
  • It is 95% Nitrogen ($N_2$) and 5% Methane ($CH_4$).
  • The surface pressure is 1.45x that of Earth. If you stood there, it would feel like being at the bottom of a swimming pool!
🟠

The Orange Smog

Where does the color come from?

Tholins

  • When ultraviolet light from the Sun hits the methane and nitrogen high in the atmosphere, it breaks them apart.
  • They recombine into complex organic molecules called Tholins, which create the opaque orange smog that hides the surface from telescopes.
🌊

Alien Oceans

Liquid on the surface!

Methane Seas

  • Titan is the only place besides Earth known to have stable liquids on its surface.
  • However, because it is -179°C, water is frozen harder than rock. The lakes are actually filled with liquid methane and ethane!
  • The largest sea is named Kraken Mare.
🌧️

The "Hydrological" Cycle

Raining hydrocarbons

Earth's Bizarre Twin

Methane Rain: Just like Earth has a water cycle, Titan has a methane cycle. Liquid methane evaporates, forms clouds, and rains back down onto the surface.
Carving the Land: This rain carves deep river channels and canyons into the "rock" (which is actually solid water ice), dumping into the polar lakes.
🦠

Astrobiology

Could life exist here?

Two Different Habitats

  • The Subsurface Ocean: Like Europa, Titan is believed to have a global ocean of liquid liquid water deep beneath its icy crust. It could support life as we know it.
  • The Surface Lakes: Some scientists theorize that completely alien, methane-based life forms could potentially evolve in the surface lakes of liquid hydrocarbons!
🪂

The Huygens Probe

The historic 2005 landing

Touching Down in the Smog

  • In 2005, the European Space Agency's Huygens probe detached from the Cassini spacecraft and parachuted through Titan's thick atmosphere.
  • It is the most distant landing of any spacecraft from Earth in history!
  • It landed in a dry riverbed filled with smooth, rounded "pebbles" made of solid water ice.
🚁

Future Mission

NASA's Dragonfly (Launching 2028)

A Nuclear Drone

  • Because Titan has a thick atmosphere and very low gravity, it is the easiest place in the solar system to fly!
  • NASA is sending Dragonfly, a car-sized, nuclear-powered dual-quadcopter.
  • Arriving in 2034, it will fly from location to location, sampling the organic sands and searching for the chemical building blocks of life.
