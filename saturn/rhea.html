Size & Position

Saturn's #2

The Second Largest

  • Rhea is the second-largest moon of Saturn (after Titan) and the 9th largest moon in the entire solar system.
  • It orbits Saturn at a distance of 527,000 km, taking about 4.5 Earth days to complete one orbit.
⛄

Composition

A giant dirty snowball

Low Density Ice

  • Rhea has a very low density (1.23 g/cm³).
  • This tells scientists it is composed of roughly 75% water ice and 25% rock. It is basically a giant, frozen, dirty snowball floating in space.
🕳️

The Surface

Ancient scars

Heavily Cratered

  • Like Callisto, Rhea's leading hemisphere is extremely heavily cratered, meaning it hasn't had any geological activity to erase them for billions of years.
  • It features two massive impact basins named Tirawa and Mamaldi.
⚡

Wispy Terrain

What are those bright lines?

Giant Ice Cliffs

  • Voyager 1 photographed bright, "wispy" lines on Rhea's trailing hemisphere.
  • The Cassini probe later flew close enough to reveal these aren't clouds or paint—they are massive tectonic fault lines and ice cliffs (chasmata) created when the moon cooled and cracked!
🪐

The Ring Mystery

Did a moon have its own rings?

A False Alarm

The 2008 Claim: Cassini sensors detected a weird drop in electrons around Rhea, leading scientists to announce it might be the first moon to have a ring system!
The 2010 Truth: Cassini did a targeted flyby specifically to photograph these rings... but found absolutely nothing. The electron drop remains a mystery, but there are no rings.
💨

Oxygen Atmosphere!

Wait, can we breathe there?

Radiation-Created Air

  • In 2010, NASA confirmed Rhea has a very thin exosphere made of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide.
  • No, you can't breathe it—it is trillions of times thinner than Earth's.
  • It isn't from plants! It is created by radiolysis: Saturn's radiation bombards Rhea's surface, literally splitting the water ice molecules apart into oxygen and hydrogen!
🔭

Discovery

The Stars of Louis

Giovanni Cassini

  • Discovered in 1672 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini.
  • He originally named it and three other moons the Sidera Lodoicea ("Stars of Louis") to flatter King Louis XIV of France. The name Rhea was adopted much later!

The Grand Tour

  • First photographed by the Voyager 1 and 2 probes in 1980 and 1981, which discovered the "wispy terrain".
  • The Cassini spacecraft performed several targeted close flybys between 2005 and 2015, solving the mystery of the ice cliffs and detecting the oxygen atmosphere!
