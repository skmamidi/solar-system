The Shape

Why isn't it round?

Failed Sphere

  • Proteus is the largest known irregular (non-spherical) body in the solar system.
  • It measures about 420 km across. If it were just slightly larger, its own gravity would have been strong enough to crush it into a perfect sphere!
🕶️

The Glare

Hidden in plain sight

Blinded by Neptune

  • Proteus wasn't discovered from Earth because it orbits incredibly close to Neptune.
  • Combined with its dark surface, the bright blue glare of Neptune completely blinded telescopes, hiding the moon until a probe flew close!
💥

Pharos Crater

A devastating impact

A Near-Fatal Blow

  • Proteus is heavily cratered, but one stands out: Pharos.
  • This crater is over 230 km wide—more than half the diameter of the entire moon! The impact that created it nearly shattered Proteus to pieces.
🧊

Composition

What is it made of?

Porous Ice and Rock

  • It is believed to be made of roughly equal amounts of water ice and dark silicate rock.
  • It has a very low density, meaning it is likely highly porous (full of holes inside) like a giant space sponge.
🌑

Albedo (Reflectivity)

As dark as soot

Swallowing Light

Pitch Black: Proteus reflects only 9.6% of the sunlight that hits it. It is one of the darkest objects in the solar system, appearing as dark as soot.
Color: Its surface is a neutral, deep gray. It shows no signs of geological activity or fresh ice to brighten it up.
🔄

Orbit

The normal moon

A Native Son

Unlike Triton, which was captured from the Kuiper belt and orbits backwards, Proteus is a "regular" moon. It formed out of the debris disk surrounding Neptune.
It orbits in the prograde direction (the same way Neptune spins) directly above Neptune's equator.
🛰️

Discovery

Found by a robot

Voyager 2 (1989)

  • Because of the intense glare of Neptune, Proteus was completely invisible to Earth-based telescopes.
  • It was finally discovered in 1989 by Stephen P. Synnott from the images beamed back by the Voyager 2 spacecraft!
  • Voyager 2 remains the only spacecraft to have ever visited Proteus.
🌊

Mythology

What does the name mean?

The Shapeshifter

  • Following the convention of naming Neptunian moons after water deities, it is named after Proteus, a shape-shifting sea god from Greek mythology.
  • This is highly appropriate, given the moon's strange, irregular, non-spherical shape!
