Discovery

A magical trick of light

Stellar Occultation

  • Larissa was discovered in 1981 not by seeing it directly, but because it blocked the light of a distant star!
  • Astronomers measuring the star's brightness saw a dip. This process is called occultation.
  • It was officially confirmed by Voyager 2 in 1989.
🥔

Size & Shape

Is it a perfect sphere?

An Irregular Potato

  • Larissa is about 194 kilometers in diameter, making it Neptune's fourth-largest moon.
  • Because it lacks sufficient mass, its gravity cannot crush it into a sphere, leaving it looking like an irregular, lumpy potato.
☄️

The Surface

What does it look like?

Heavily Cratered

  • Voyager 2 photos show that Larissa is heavily cratered.
  • Unlike Triton, which has active geysers wiping its surface clean, Larissa appears completely dead and shows no signs of geological modification.
🌑

Albedo

Is it bright or dark?

A Dark World

  • Larissa is very dark, similar to Proteus.
  • It reflects less than 10% of the sunlight that hits it. This made it incredibly difficult to spot against the bright glare of Neptune!
🔄

The Orbit

A perilous path

Below Synchronous Orbit

Fast Flyer: Larissa orbits incredibly close to Neptune, zipping around the giant planet in just over 13 hours!
The Drag: Because it orbits faster than Neptune spins, tidal forces from the planet are acting like a brake, slowly dragging the moon inward over millions of years.
💥

Ultimate Fate

What will happen to it?

Destruction by Gravity

  • As Larissa spirals closer to Neptune, it will eventually cross the Roche limit.
  • At this distance, Neptune's gravitational pull on the near side of the moon will be so much stronger than on the far side that it will literally tear the moon to pieces!
  • These pieces will spread out and form a brand new planetary ring around Neptune.
🛰️

Voyager 2

The only visitor

Confirmation at Last

  • For 8 years after the 1981 occultation, scientists weren't fully sure Larissa existed.
  • In 1989, NASA's Voyager 2 flew through the Neptune system and finally captured the first direct photographs of Larissa, proving the occultation data was correct!
🌊

Mythology

What does the name mean?

The Sea Nymph

  • In Greek mythology, Larissa was a beautiful nymph of Thessaly and one of the lovers of Poseidon.
  • Since Neptune is the Roman equivalent of Poseidon, naming the moon after his mythological counterpart's lover perfectly fits the naming convention of the Neptunian system!
