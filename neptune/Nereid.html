Discovery

Who found it?

Gerard Kuiper

  • Nereid was discovered in 1949 by the famous astronomer Gerard Kuiper (the namesake of the Kuiper Belt!).
  • It was the very last moon of Neptune discovered using Earth-based telescopes before Voyager 2 arrived.
🎢

The Orbit

A solar system record

Most Eccentric

  • Nereid holds the record for the most eccentric (elliptical) orbit of any moon in the solar system.
  • At its closest, it is 1.3 million km from Neptune. At its farthest, it swings out to 9.6 million km!
🥔

Size & Shape

Is it a sphere?

The Lumpy Ice Ball

  • With a diameter of about 340 km, it is the third-largest of Neptune's 14 moons.
  • Despite its size, it is irregularly shaped (like a lumpy potato), meaning its gravity isn't strong enough to pull it into a perfect sphere.
⏳

Length of Year

A long journey

360 Days

  • Because its orbit swings so far away from Neptune, it takes a very long time to complete a single lap.
  • One orbit for Nereid takes 360 Earth days—nearly a full Earth year just to go around its planet once!
🧊

Composition

What is it made of?

Water Ice and Rock

A Frozen Mix: Spectral data indicates that Nereid is composed of a mixture of water ice and some silicate rock.
Surface Neutrality: Unlike other outer moons that get stained red by tholins or radiation, Nereid appears very neutral in color (grey/white).
🤔

The Origin Mystery

Why is its orbit so crazy?

Two Leading Theories

Theory 1: Captured! Like Triton, it might be a Kuiper Belt object that drifted too close and was captured by Neptune's gravity.
Theory 2: Disrupted! It formed near Neptune, but when Neptune violently captured Triton, the gravitational chaos flung Nereid out into its extreme orbit!
🛰️

Voyager 2 Flyby

The only visitor

A Long-Distance Snapshot

  • NASA's Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have ever visited the Neptune system (in 1989).
  • Because Nereid was at the far end of its eccentric orbit, Voyager passed it at a massive distance of 4.7 million km.
  • The resulting photos were very blurry, showing only a smudged, irregular shape with no surface details!
🌊

Mythology

What does the name mean?

The Sea Nymphs

  • In Greek mythology, the Nereids were 50 sea nymphs who were the daughters of Nereus (the old man of the sea).
  • They were known to accompany Poseidon (the Roman equivalent is Neptune), making it a perfect name for a moon circling the blue giant!
