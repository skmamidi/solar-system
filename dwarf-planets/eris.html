Discovery

Who found it first?

January 5, 2005

  • Discovered by astronomers Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo, and David Rabinowitz at Palomar Observatory.
  • Its discovery led to the debate that reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet in 2006.
⚖️

Mass vs Pluto

Why it caused a debate

27% More Massive

  • Eris is about 27% more massive than Pluto, though slightly smaller in volume.
  • This unexpected size sparked a debate: if Pluto is a planet, shouldn't Eris be one too?
🌑

Dysnomia

Eris's moon

Discovered 2005

  • Dysnomia is Eris's only known moon, discovered shortly after Eris itself.
  • By tracking Dysnomia's orbit, astronomers precisely calculated Eris's mass for the first time.
💫

Scattered Disc

Where does Eris live?

Beyond the Kuiper Belt

  • Eris orbits in the scattered disc, a distant region even farther than the Kuiper Belt.
  • Objects here have highly elliptical orbits, likely shaped by interactions with Neptune long ago.
🔄

Orbit

A long, lonely journey

Highly Elliptical

Orbital Period: Eris takes about 557 to 560 years to orbit the Sun once, with an average distance of 68 AU.
Extreme Distance: At its farthest, Eris is about 98 AU from the Sun—almost three times farther than Pluto's average distance.
🧊

Composition

Rock and frozen gases

Rocky Core + Ice

  • Eris has a rocky core beneath a thick surface of nitrogen ice.
  • Its frozen atmosphere may collapse onto the surface as it moves farther from the Sun.
  • Scientists believe methane ice may also be present on its extremely cold surface.
✨

Surface

Almost as bright as snow

Extremely Reflective

  • Eris reflects about 96% of the light that hits it, almost as bright as fresh snow.
  • This high reflectivity (albedo) suggests its surface is covered in fresh, unweathered nitrogen ice.
🏛️

Naming

Why "Eris"?

Goddess of Discord

  • Eris is named after the Greek goddess of discord and strife, a fitting name for an object that sparked a scientific uproar.
  • Its moon Dysnomia is named after the spirit of lawlessness, the daughter of Eris.
