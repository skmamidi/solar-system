Discovery

Who found it first?

2005 Discovery

  • Discovered by Mike Brown's team at Caltech on March 31, 2005, shortly after Easter.
  • It was announced in July 2005, around the same time as Eris.
🌑

Moon MK2

A dark companion

Discovered 2015

  • Makemake's moon, nicknamed MK2, was discovered by the Hubble Space Telescope in 2015.
  • It is unusually dark, suggesting it may be covered in charcoal-like material.
🔴

Red Color

Why so reddish?

Frozen Methane

  • Frozen methane and organic compounds called tholins give Makemake its reddish-brown color.
  • Tholins are created when sunlight breaks apart simple molecules like methane over billions of years.
❄️

Methane Ice

Surface composition

Frozen Pellets

  • Makemake's surface is covered in large pellets of frozen methane, possibly centimeters across.
  • It also has nitrogen ice, similar to Pluto, but in much smaller amounts.
🌌

Kuiper Belt

Home in the outer solar system

Classical KBO

Location: Makemake orbits in the classical Kuiper Belt at an average distance of 45.8 AU from the Sun.
Orbit: Unlike Pluto, Makemake has a relatively circular orbit with low eccentricity and no Neptune resonance.
🔄

Orbit

A slow journey around the Sun

306 Year Period

  • Makemake takes 306 Earth years to complete one orbit around the Sun.
  • Its orbit has low eccentricity compared to Pluto, meaning it stays at a more consistent distance.
  • The orbit is tilted about 29 degrees relative to Earth's orbital plane.
🏝️

Naming

A name from Easter Island

Rapa Nui Creator God

  • Makemake is named after the creator god of the Rapa Nui people of Easter Island.
  • It is pronounced mah-kee-mah-kee. The name was chosen because it was discovered around Easter 2005.
🌑

No Atmosphere

Too small and too cold

Airless World

  • Makemake is too small and too cold to hold onto a significant atmosphere.
  • Unlike Pluto, which has a thin atmosphere, Makemake's gravity is too weak to keep gases from escaping into space.
