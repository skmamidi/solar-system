Discovery

Who found it first?

February 18, 1930

  • Discovered by American astronomer Clyde Tombaugh at Lowell Observatory in Arizona.
  • A young 24-year-old Tombaugh found it by painstakingly comparing photographic plates.
❤️

Tombaugh Regio

The famous heart

Heart of Pluto

  • A vast heart-shaped nitrogen ice plain on Pluto's surface, named after Clyde Tombaugh.
  • It is smooth, young geologically, and formed by convection in the nitrogen ice sheet.
💨

Atmosphere

A disappearing veil

Thin & Seasonal

  • Made of nitrogen, methane, and carbon monoxide.
  • As Pluto moves farther from the Sun, its atmosphere freezes and collapses onto the surface, only to sublimate back when it gets closer.
🌙

Moons

Five companions

5 Known Moons

  • Charon is by far the largest, nearly half Pluto's size.
  • The others are tiny: Styx, Nix, Kerberos, and Hydra.
🚀

New Horizons

The mission that flew by

July 14, 2015 Flyby

First Close-Up: NASA's New Horizons gave us our first close-up images of Pluto and Charon after a 9.5-year journey.
Stunning Details: It revealed mountains of water ice, nitrogen glaciers, blue hazes in the atmosphere, and the iconic heart.
🧊

Composition

Layers of ice and rock

Ice World

  • Pluto has a rocky core, surrounded by a mantle of water ice, and a surface coated with frozen nitrogen and methane.
  • Its mountains are made of water ice, as hard as rock at Pluto's frigid temperatures.
⚖️

Binary System

Pluto and Charon dance

Barycenter Outside Pluto

  • Pluto and Charon are so close in mass that they orbit a point in space between them called the barycenter.
  • This makes them a binary (dwarf) planet system, unique in our solar system.
📉

Demotion

From planet to dwarf

2006 IAU Reclassification

  • In 2006, the IAU reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet after the discovery of Eris, a similar-sized body in the Kuiper Belt.
  • Pluto had not "cleared its neighborhood" of other objects, one of the criteria for full planethood.
