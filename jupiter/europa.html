The Second Galilean

Where does it orbit?

Fast Orbit

  • It is the second of the four Galilean moons extending outward from Jupiter (after Io).
  • It orbits Jupiter at a distance of about 670,900 km, taking about 3.55 Earth days to complete one trip.
🔥

Tidal Heating

Why is it melting inside?

The Squeeze

  • Europa is locked in a 1:2:4 orbital resonance with Io and Ganymede.
  • This constant gravitational tug-of-war literally stretches and squishes the moon. This friction creates massive internal heat (Tidal Heating), preventing the ocean from freezing solid!
🧊

The Ice Shell

Smoothest object in space

A Young Surface

  • The solid ice crust is estimated to be 10 to 25 km thick.
  • It has almost no large impact craters. This means the surface is very young and "active"—water from below constantly erupts to freeze over and erase old scars!
⚡

Lineae

The reddish-brown scars

Cracking Ice

  • The most striking features are the lineae (dark streaks crisscrossing the globe).
  • As Jupiter's gravity flexes the moon, the ice cracks. Warmer slushy ice and salts from the ocean below well up into the cracks, turning reddish-brown when exposed to space radiation.
🌊

The Global Ocean

More water than Earth

A Deep Abyss

Magnetic Proof: The Galileo spacecraft detected that Europa induces a magnetic field, consistent with a highly conductive, salty subsurface ocean.
Massive Scale: The ocean is estimated to be up to 100 km deep across the entire moon.
🦠

Astrobiology

Could there be alien life?

The Best Candidate

  • Europa is considered one of the most likely places to find extraterrestrial life in our solar system.
  • It has all three ingredients for life: Liquid water, organic chemical building blocks, and an energy source.
  • Because sunlight can't penetrate the ice, scientists theorize that hydrothermal vents at the bottom of the ocean floor could provide heat and chemical energy for microbes, just like at the bottom of Earth's oceans!
💦

Water Plumes

Shooting into space

Erupting Geysers

  • The Hubble Space Telescope has found evidence for possible towering plumes of water vapor erupting from Europa's south polar region.
  • These suspected geysers may shoot up to 200 km into space. This is incredibly exciting because spacecraft could fly through the plumes and sample ocean material without having to drill through 20 km of solid ice!
🚀

Space Missions

Europa Clipper

The Next Great Leap

  • Past: The Galileo probe intentionally crashed into Jupiter in 2003 to ensure it wouldn't accidentally contaminate Europa with Earth microbes!
  • Active (Europa Clipper): NASA launched Europa Clipper on Oct. 14, 2024. The spacecraft will orbit Jupiter and perform 49 close flybys of Europa to scan its ocean, ice shell, and habitability.
