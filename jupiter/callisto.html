The Outcast

Where does it orbit?

The Farthest Galilean

  • It is the outermost of the four Galilean moons discovered by Galileo in 1610.
  • It orbits Jupiter at a distance of about 1.88 million km, taking 16.7 days to complete one trip.
🎼

Orbital Dance

Why is it dead?

No Tidal Heating

  • Unlike Io, Europa, and Ganymede, Callisto is NOT locked in an orbital resonance.
  • Because it isn't constantly being tugged by the other moons, it doesn't experience "tidal heating." This is why its interior never fully melted!
☄️

The Surface

A record-breaking landscape

The Crater King

  • Callisto is the most heavily cratered object in the entire solar system.
  • Because it has no geological activity (no volcanoes or shifting ice), every single asteroid strike over the last 4 billion years has left a permanent scar.
🎯

Valhalla Basin

The giant target

A Massive Impact

  • The largest feature on Callisto is the Valhalla multi-ring impact crater.
  • When a massive asteroid hit it billions of years ago, the slushy ice rippled outward and froze, creating concentric rings that stretch up to 3,800 km across!
🍧

Internal Structure

The "Undifferentiated" Slushy

A Mixed-Up World

No Clean Layers: Most planets have an iron core, a rocky mantle, and a crust. Callisto is undifferentiated (or only partially differentiated).
A Giant Snowball: Because it never fully melted, its rock and ice are mostly just mixed together uniformly throughout its entire interior!
👨‍🚀

Human Exploration

The best place to build a base?

The Safe Zone

  • Jupiter is surrounded by intense, deadly radiation belts that would kill an astronaut on Europa or Io in minutes.
  • Because Callisto orbits so far away from Jupiter, it sits in a low radiation zone.
  • NASA's HOPE study concluded that Callisto is the absolute best candidate for a future human surface base in the Jovian system, using its ice for water and rocket fuel!
🛰️

Space Missions

Who has visited?

Probing the Craters

  • Past: The Voyager probes gave us our first clear photos. The Galileo spacecraft performed several close flybys in the 1990s.
  • Upcoming: ESA's JUICE mission (arriving in 2031) will perform 21 flybys of Callisto to map its surface and confirm if it has a deep, hidden ocean!
🌊

A Hidden Ocean?

Wait, does it have water?

Magnetic Clues

  • Even though it is a frozen, dead world on the outside, data from the Galileo spacecraft showed its magnetic field fluctuates in a way that suggests a conductive liquid inside.
  • Scientists believe there might be a vast, salty ocean hiding extremely deep (250 km) beneath its thick crust, kept from freezing by radioactive decay in the rocks!
