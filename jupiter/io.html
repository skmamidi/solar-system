The Inner Galilean

Where does it orbit?

Extremely Close

  • It is the innermost of Jupiter's four large Galilean moons.
  • It orbits at a distance of 422,000 km, zipping around the massive gas giant in just 42.5 hours!
🍕

The Pizza Moon

Why does it look like that?

Zero Craters!

  • Unlike our Moon, Io has almost zero impact craters!
  • The surface is constantly repaved by fresh lava.
  • The vibrant yellow, orange, and red colors come from sulfur and sulfur dioxide frost covering the surface.
🔥

Tidal Heating

What causes the volcanoes?

The Great Squish

  • Io is caught in a gravitational tug-of-war between massive Jupiter on one side, and Europa/Ganymede on the other.
  • This constant pulling flexes the solid crust of Io up and down by up to 100 meters! This intense friction melts the interior rock.
🌋

Most Volcanic

A record-breaking world

A Hellish Landscape

  • Io is the most volcanically active body in the solar system.
  • It has over 400 active volcanoes. Some plumes blast sulfur dioxide gas up to 500 km (300 miles) into space!
🌊

Loki Patera

The largest volcano on Io

A Giant Lava Lake

Massive Size: Loki Patera is a volcanic depression (caldera) over 200 km across!
Overturning Crust: It is an active lava lake where the solid crust frequently founders and sinks, exposing brilliant fresh magma underneath.
⚡

The Plasma Torus

Feeding Jupiter's radiation belts

Electric Donut

  • As Io's volcanoes erupt, Jupiter's massive magnetic field sweeps past the moon, stripping away 1 ton of sulfur and oxygen ions every single second!
  • This material forms a giant, glowing, donut-shaped ring of lethal radiation around Jupiter called the Io Plasma Torus.
  • This radiation makes it incredibly dangerous for spacecraft to fly near Io.
💨

Atmosphere

The collapsing sky

Freezing in the Dark

  • Io has an extremely thin atmosphere composed almost entirely of Sulfur Dioxide gas ($SO_2$) belched from volcanoes.
  • Every day, when Io passes into Jupiter's cold shadow (an eclipse), the temperature drops so low that the entire atmosphere freezes solid and falls to the ground! When the sun returns, it sublimates back into gas.
🚀

Space Missions

The great discovery

Voyager's Shock

  • Voyager 1 (1979): Before Voyager, scientists thought moons were dead, cratered rocks. Navigational engineer Linda Morabito noticed a weird bump on the edge of Io in a photo... it was a 300km high volcanic plume!
  • Juno (2023-2024): The Juno spacecraft recently performed incredibly close flybys, capturing stunning high-resolution images of fresh lava lakes and rugged mountains.
