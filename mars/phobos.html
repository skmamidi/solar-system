The Inner Martian Moon

Phobos

"The Doomed Moon"

The larger and innermost of Mars' two moons. It is a heavily cratered, lumpy rock that orbits so incredibly close to Mars that it zips around the planet three times a day. Unfortunately, it is slowly spiraling inward to its doom!

Position & Distance

How close is it?

Hugging the Planet

Phobos orbits only 6,000 km (3,700 miles) above the Martian surface. It is the closest moon to its primary planet in the entire solar system!

🥔

Size & Shape

Why isn't it round?

The Lumpy Potato

  • It is roughly 22 km across. Larger than Deimos, but still incredibly small.
  • Like Deimos, it lacks the massive gravity needed to pull itself into a perfect sphere, leaving it lumpy and irregular.
☄️

Stickney Crater

The giant scar

A Near-Fatal Blow

  • Phobos is dominated by a massive 9 km wide crater named Stickney.
  • The impact that created it was so violent that it almost shattered the entire moon! It left huge stress-fracture grooves radiating across the surface.
🪶

Microgravity

Can you jump off it?

Light as a Feather

  • A 150 lb person on Earth would weigh about 2 ounces on Phobos.
  • The escape velocity is 11.3 m/s. You probably couldn't jump into space (unlike on Deimos), but you could easily jump over a house!
🔄

Bizarre Orbit

Rising in the wrong direction!

Faster Than The Planet

The Speed: Mars takes 24h 37m to rotate. Phobos orbits Mars in just 7h 39m. It orbits faster than the planet spins!
The Result: Because it overtakes the planet's rotation, to an observer on Mars, Phobos rises in the West and sets in the East twice a day!
💥

The Doomed Moon

What is its ultimate fate?

Spiraling to Destruction

  • Because it orbits faster than Mars rotates, tidal forces are dragging it backward, causing its orbit to shrink by about 1.8 meters every 100 years.
  • In roughly 50 million years, one of two things will happen:
  • 1. It will crash into the Martian surface.
  • 2. (More likely) The gravitational pull will tear it apart, creating a spectacular rocky ring around Mars!
🚀

Space Missions

Who is visiting Phobos?

The MMX Mission

  • Past Failures: The Soviet Union and Russia sent several probes (Phobos 1, 2, and Phobos-Grunt) to land, but all failed or were lost.
  • JAXA's MMX: Japan's Martian Moons eXploration mission is scheduled for JFY 2026 to study both Martian moons, land on Phobos, collect rock samples, and return them to Earth.
🏛️

Mythology

What does the name mean?

The God of Fear

  • Mars is named after the Roman god of war (Ares in Greek mythology).
  • In Greek myths, Ares was accompanied into battle by his twin sons.
  • Phobos is the god of Fear and Panic. (This is where the English word "Phobia" comes from!)
