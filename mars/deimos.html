Orbit & Fate

Where is it going?

Drifting Away

Deimos orbits 23,460 km above Mars. While its twin brother Phobos is doomed to crash into Mars, Deimos is actually slowly drifting further away from the planet, just like Earth's moon!

🥔

Size & Shape

Why isn't it round?

The Space Potato

  • It is only about 12.4 km across. You could walk around it in an afternoon.
  • Because it is so incredibly small, it doesn't have enough gravity to pull itself into a sphere, leaving it lumpy!
🪶

Extreme Gravity

Jumping into space

Escape Velocity

Gravity on Deimos is just 0.0003 times Earth's.

The escape velocity is 5.6 m/s (12.5 mph). If you sprinted and jumped off a small ramp, you would leave the moon and float into deep space forever!

🧹

The Surface

Why is it so smooth?

Smothered in Dust

  • Unlike Phobos, Deimos looks very smooth.
  • Because gravity is so weak, dust knocked up by meteor impacts (regolith) takes a long time to fall back, slowly hiding all craters over millions of years!
🪨

Composition & Origin

Where did it come from?

The Great Debate

Captured Asteroid? Made of carbon-rich rock (C-type), which led scientists to believe it was an asteroid from Jupiter's belt captured by Mars.
Or Martian Chunk? New data suggests it isn't an asteroid! It may have formed from a giant impact that blasted chunks of Mars itself into orbit.
🌑

Solar Eclipses

Can Deimos block the Sun?

A Tiny Black Dot

  • Deimos is so incredibly small and far away from the surface that it cannot cover the Sun like our Moon does.
  • When it passes in front of the Sun, it looks like a tiny, lumpy black dot crossing the sky. This is called a Transit.
🛰️

Space Missions

Who is visiting Deimos?

Exploring the Twins

  • UAE Hope Probe (2023): Flew within 100km of Deimos, taking the highest resolution photos ever.
  • Upcoming MMX Mission: Japan's JAXA-led MMX mission is scheduled for JFY 2026 to study both Martian moons, including close observations of Deimos, and help solve their origin.
🏛️

Mythology

What does the name mean?

Dread and Terror

  • Mars is named after the Roman god of war (Ares in Greek mythology).
  • In the myths, Ares was accompanied into battle by his twin sons.
  • The moons are named after them: Phobos (Fear) and Deimos (Dread and Terror).
