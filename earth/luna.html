The Near Side

Why do we always see the same face?

Tidal Locking

The Moon takes 27.3 days to orbit Earth, and exactly 27.3 days to rotate once on its axis! Because these speeds match perfectly, it is Tidally Locked, meaning we only ever see one side of it from Earth.

📏

Relative Size

How big is it?

A Quarter of Earth

  • Diameter: 3,474 km. It is roughly 1/4 the width of Earth.
  • Ranking: It is the 5th largest moon in the entire solar system.
💥

Formation

Where did it come from?

The Giant Impact

The leading theory is the Giant Impact Hypothesis. About 4.5 billion years ago, a Mars-sized planet named Theia smashed into the young Earth. The debris from this violent collision clumped together in orbit to form the Moon!

🪨

Composition

What is Moon dust?

Regolith & Glass

  • The surface is covered in a fine, powdery dust called Regolith.
  • Because there is no wind or water to erode it, regolith particles are jagged and sharp like tiny shards of glass. It easily ruins spacesuits!
🌊

The Moon's Power

Tides, Eclipses, and Wobbles

Invisible Influence

Ocean Tides: The Moon's gravity pulls on Earth's oceans, creating high and low tides. (The Sun helps too!)
Perfect Eclipses: The Sun is 400x wider than the Moon, but also exactly 400x further away. This coincidence allows the Moon to perfectly block the Sun during a Total Solar Eclipse!
🔇

The Environment

The Silent Vacuum

A Dead World

  • Atmosphere: Almost non-existent. It has a super-thin "exosphere" of atoms kicked up by solar wind.
  • Sound: Because there is no air, there is absolutely no sound on the Moon. Astronauts had to use radios even when standing next to each other.
  • Footprints: Without wind or rain to wash them away, the footprints left by the Apollo astronauts will remain there for millions of years.
👨‍🚀

The Apollo Era

One Giant Leap (1969 - 1972)

The First Explorers

  • Apollo 11 (1969): Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to ever walk on another celestial body.
  • The Elite Dozen: During the Apollo program, a total of exactly 12 American astronauts walked on the lunar surface.
  • The Science: They brought back 842 lbs of lunar rocks, which proved the Giant Impact Hypothesis and revolutionized planetary science.
🚀

The Artemis Generation

Returning to stay

To The South Pole

  • The Goal: NASA's new Artemis program will return humans to the Moon, including the first woman and first person of color.
  • The Destination: Unlike Apollo, Artemis will land at the Lunar South Pole.
  • Why the pole? Deep craters at the poles are in permanent shadow and contain massive amounts of water ice, which can be used for drinking water and rocket fuel!
