Size & Position

Where does it orbit?

The Smallest Sibling

  • Miranda is the innermost and smallest of Uranus's five major, round moons.
  • It orbits incredibly close to Uranus, completing a full orbit in just 1.4 Earth days!
🧟

Frankenstein

Why does it look so weird?

A Patchwork Surface

  • Its surface is a bizarre jumble of heavily cratered plains crossed by massive, ridged fault lines and deep canyons.
  • It literally looks like pieces of different moons were glued together. This is why it is nicknamed the Frankenstein Moon!
💥

Formation Theories

How did the scars form?

Shattered or Stretched?

  • Theory 1: A massive asteroid completely shattered the moon, and the pieces slowly clumped back together randomly.
  • Theory 2 (Current favorite): Extreme tidal heating caused partially melted slush inside to erupt and stretch the crust apart, creating tectonic scars.
🏁

Coronae

Giant racetracks

Oval Grooves

  • The most distinctive features on Miranda are three massive, oval-shaped grooved structures called coronae.
  • They look like giant, 200km-wide thumbprints or race tracks!
  • They are named Arden, Elsinore, and Inverness (from Shakespeare's plays).
☀️

Extreme Seasons

The side effects of a sideways planet

42-Year Winters

Because Uranus rolls on its side (98° tilt), its moons orbit along its equator perpendicular to the sun.
This means Miranda's poles spend 42 Earth years in continuous sunlight, followed by 42 Earth years of total, freezing darkness!
🧗‍♂️

Verona Rupes

The tallest cliff in the solar system

A 20-Kilometer Drop

  • Miranda features a massive fault scarp called Verona Rupes.
  • It is a sheer vertical drop of roughly 20 kilometers (12 miles). It is the tallest known cliff in the entire solar system!
  • Because Miranda's gravity is so incredibly weak (0.008g), if you jumped off Verona Rupes, it would take you 12 entire minutes to hit the bottom!
🛰️

Voyager 2

The only visitor

A Brief Encounter

  • The NASA Voyager 2 probe is the only spacecraft to have ever visited the Uranian system, making a flyby in January 1986.
  • To get a gravity assist toward Neptune, Voyager 2 had to fly incredibly close to Miranda (just 29,000 km away), giving us our sharpest, most breathtaking photos of any Uranian moon!
🧊

Composition

What is it made of?

Half Ice, Half Rock

  • Like Ariel, Miranda's density suggests it consists of roughly equal parts water ice and silicate rock.
  • Unlike the larger moons, scientists believe Miranda may not be fully differentiated (meaning its rock and ice are still somewhat mixed together instead of having a solid rocky core).
