Position & Discovery

The farthest out

William Herschel

  • Discovered in 1787 by William Herschel (on the same day he discovered Titania!).
  • It is the outermost of Uranus's five major moons, orbiting at a distance of about 583,500 km.
☄️

Ancient Surface

Why so many craters?

Geologically Dead

  • Because it orbits so far out, Oberon experiences very little tidal heating from Uranus.
  • As a result, it is geologically dead. Its surface is the oldest and most heavily cratered of all the Uranian moons.
📖

Mythology & Naming

King of the Fairies

A Midsummer Night's Dream

  • Like all of Uranus's moons, Oberon is named after a character from English literature.
  • Oberon is the mythical King of the Fairies in William Shakespeare's play A Midsummer Night's Dream.
🧊

Composition

Ice and Rock

Differentiated Core

  • Oberon consists of roughly equal parts water ice and dense rock.
  • Scientists believe it is fully differentiated, meaning the heavy rock sank to form a solid core, surrounded by a thick, icy mantle.
🕳️

The Dark Crater Mystery

What is hiding inside?

Flooded Depressions

The Feature: Many of the largest craters on Oberon (like Hamlet, Macduff, and Othello) have mysterious, pitch-black material covering their floors.
The Theory: Scientists believe that after a meteor cracked the crust, dirty, slushy water from inside the moon upwelled into the crater and froze, creating dark "ice lava" lakes!
⛰️

Mount Mommur

The massive peak on the edge

A Towering Discovery

  • In the Voyager 2 photos, a massive mountain can be seen sticking out from the bottom edge (the limb) of Oberon.
  • It is estimated to be 6 kilometers (20,000 feet) high!
  • It is unofficially named Mount Mommur, after the mythical forest home of the fairy king Oberon.
  • Oberon is also crossed by giant rift valleys (canyons) called Chasmata, created when the moon's interior froze and expanded, cracking the crust.
🛰️

Voyager 2 Flyby

The only close look

A Half-Seen World

  • NASA's Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have ever visited Oberon, passing by in January 1986.
  • Because Uranus and its moons are tilted 98 degrees, the northern hemisphere of Oberon was in a 42-year dark winter. Voyager 2 could only map 40% of the moon (the southern half)!
🛡️

Outside the Shield

Leaving the magnetosphere

Exposed to the Solar Wind

  • Because Oberon orbits so incredibly far from Uranus, it actually travels outside of Uranus's protective magnetosphere during parts of its orbit!
  • This leaves its surface completely exposed to the harsh radiation of the solar wind, which slowly darkens the ice over millions of years.
