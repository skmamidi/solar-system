Discovery

How was it found?

September 22, 2004

  • Discovered by the same team at Palomar Observatory that found Orcus and Quaoar.
  • It was not officially named until 2011, spending years known only by its provisional designation.
🌑

Extremely Dark

A stealthy world

Low Albedo

  • Salacia reflects only about 4% of the sunlight that hits it—darker than charcoal!
  • This extremely low albedo means its surface is either very ancient or covered in dark organic compounds.
🔄

Binary System

Two bodies dancing

A Cosmic Waltz

  • Salacia and Actaea orbit a common center of mass, making them a true binary system.
  • Actaea is about 1/4 to 1/3 the size of Salacia, one of the largest relative moons in the Kuiper Belt.
🏷️

Name Origin

Roman sea goddess

Goddess of Salt Water

  • Salacia was the Roman goddess of salt water and the wife of Neptune, god of the sea.
  • The name connects it to Neptune, the planet whose gravity dominates the region where Salacia orbits.
⚖️

Size & Mass

A heavy lightweight

Dense for Its Size

Estimated Diameter: About 850-900 km, making it one of the largest known classical Kuiper Belt Objects.
System Mass: Combined with Actaea, the system is surprisingly massive, suggesting a high density for a KBO.
🌌

Orbital Path

A long, slow journey

Classical KBO

  • Salacia orbits at about 46 AU from the Sun with a nearly circular path.
  • Its orbit takes roughly 273 Earth years to complete.
  • It is a "classical" KBO, meaning it was not scattered by Neptune's gravity and has stayed in a stable orbit.
🌊

Moon Actaea

The sea nymph

A Nereid of Greek Myth

  • Actaea is named after one of the 50 Nereids, sea nymphs who attended Poseidon (the Greek equivalent of Neptune).
  • The moon is estimated to be about 200-300 km in diameter.
  • Actaea orbits Salacia at a distance of about 5,600 km.
📊

Among the Largest

Where does it rank?

Top Ten KBO

  • Salacia is among the top 10 largest known Kuiper Belt Objects by diameter.
  • However, because it is so dark, it was not discovered until 2004 despite its size.
  • Brighter objects like Varuna, which is smaller, were found earlier because they reflect more light.
