Discovery

How was it found?

A California Discovery

  • Discovered on June 4, 2002 by astronomers Chad Trujillo and Michael Brown at Palomar Observatory in California.
  • It was one of the largest Kuiper Belt Objects found since Pluto and Charon.
🪐

The Ring Mystery

Rings where they shouldn't be

Defying Theory

  • In 2023, scientists discovered Quaoar has a ring system located outside its Roche limit.
  • Classical physics says rings should not survive there—they should clump into moons!
  • This discovery is forcing astronomers to rewrite ring theory.
🏷️

Name Origin

Who is Quaoar?

The Creator

  • Quaoar is the creator deity of the Tongva people, Native Americans from the Los Angeles Basin area.
  • His name is pronounced "KWA-war" and he is said to have sung the world into existence.
🌑

Moon Weywot

Quaoar's companion

The Sky God

  • Weywot is Quaoar's son in Tongva mythology and the god of the sky.
  • The moon is very small—only about 80 km across—and orbits very close to Quaoar.
⚖️

Size & Density

A lightweight giant

Half of Pluto

Diameter: At about 1,110 km, Quaoar is roughly half the diameter of Pluto, making it one of the largest known KBOs.
Low Density: Quaoar has a surprisingly low density, suggesting it contains a lot of ice mixed with rock rather than being solid rock.
🔄

Nearly Circular Orbit

A gentle path around the Sun

Stable and Predictable

  • Quaoar orbits the Sun in a nearly perfect circle, unlike Pluto's more elliptical path.
  • Its orbit is also only slightly tilted relative to the planets, making it a "classical" Kuiper Belt Object.
  • A single orbit takes about 288 Earth years to complete.
🪨

Dwarf Planet Candidate

Why isn't it official yet?

On the Edge

  • Quaoar is massive enough to be a dwarf planet, but the IAU has not yet officially classified it as one.
  • It is large enough that gravity has pulled it into a roughly spherical shape.
  • Many astronomers consider it a "candidate dwarf planet" due to its size and shape.
🧊

Surface & Composition

What is it made of?

Icy and Dark

  • Spectroscopy shows Quaoar's surface contains crystalline water ice and possibly methane ice.
  • It also has traces of tholins, giving it a slightly reddish hue similar to other large KBOs.
  • The presence of crystalline ice (rather than amorphous) suggests some surface processing has occurred.
