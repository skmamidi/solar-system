The Anti-Pluto

A dark twin

Mirror Image

  • Orcus shares Pluto's 2:3 orbital resonance with Neptune, but is always on the opposite side of the Sun.
  • This makes it a kind of "anti-Pluto"—similar but opposite in position.
🔄

2:3 Resonance

Neptune's dance partner

Locked in Time

  • For every 2 orbits Neptune makes, Orcus completes exactly 3 orbits.
  • This gravitational lock prevents Orcus from ever colliding with Neptune, even though their orbits cross.
  • Pluto is locked in the exact same resonance!
🌑

Moon Vanth

A large companion

The Etruscan Psychopomp

  • Vanth is named after an Etruscan spirit who guided souls to the underworld.
  • It is unusually large relative to Orcus—about 1/3 the size of its parent body.
  • Vanth may have formed from the same collision that shaped Orcus.
🏷️

Name Origin

Roman god of the underworld

Lord of Oaths

  • Orcus was a Roman god of the underworld, specifically punisher of broken oaths.
  • The name was chosen to complement Pluto (another underworld god) and reflect their linked but opposite orbits.
🧊

Composition

Ice and rock in layers

Differentiated Body

Rocky Core: Orcus likely has a dense rocky core surrounded by a thick mantle of water ice.
Icy Surface: The surface contains water ice, methane, and ethane, with tholins giving it a neutral gray color.
🔭

Discovery

Found by the Palomar team

February 17, 2004

  • Discovered by the same team that found Quaoar: Michael Brown, Chad Trujillo, and David Rabinowitz.
  • They used the Samuel Oschin Telescope at Palomar Observatory.
  • Its large moon Vanth was discovered just two years later in 2005.
⚖️

Orcus vs Pluto

Similar but different

Twins of the Deep

  • Both are in Neptune's 2:3 resonance, both have large moons, and both are made of ice and rock.
  • However, Orcus is smaller (~910 km vs Pluto's 2,377 km), much darker, and lacks Pluto's complex atmosphere and geology.
  • Where Pluto is dynamic and active, Orcus appears geologically dead.
🌌

Orbital Path

Tilted and eccentric

A Wandering Path

  • Orcus's orbit is moderately eccentric, bringing it from about 30 AU to 48 AU from the Sun.
  • Its orbit is also tilted about 21 degrees relative to the plane of the planets.
  • Because of the resonance, it always stays at least 17 AU away from Neptune, never coming close enough to collide.
