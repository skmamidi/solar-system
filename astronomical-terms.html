The Olympian's Dictionary

Tap cards to flip. Study the Olympiad Facts and added coordinate-system terms for the exam!

⬆️

Zenith

The point directly overhead

Zenith

The point on the celestial sphere that is exactly, 100% directly over an observer's head.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

In the altitude-azimuth system, the Zenith has an altitude of exactly +90°. The point diametrically opposite the Zenith (directly under your feet, passing through Earth) is called the Nadir (altitude -90°).

🌅

Horizon

Where sky & Earth meet

Horizon

The great circle halfway between the zenith and the nadir, acting as the boundary between the Earth and the sky.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

The Celestial Horizon (or Astronomical Horizon) assumes a perfectly flat Earth without mountains or buildings. It always sits at exactly 0° altitude. Azimuth is measured along the horizon (0° to 360°).

🌐

Celestial Meridian

The sky's dividing line

Celestial Meridian

An imaginary line passing from the North Celestial Pole, straight through your Zenith, to the South Celestial Pole.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

When an object crosses this line, it's called a Transit or Culmination (its highest point in the sky). "AM" means Ante Meridiem (before crossing the meridian) and "PM" means Post Meridiem!

🧭

Celestial Poles

The pivot points of the sky

Celestial Poles

The two points in the sky directly above Earth's North and South poles. The entire sky appears to rotate around them.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

The altitude of the visible Celestial Pole exactly equals your latitude on Earth!

Due to Earth's wobble (Precession), the poles shift. Polaris won't always be the North Star!

➗

Celestial Equator

Earth's belt, projected

Celestial Equator

An imaginary great circle on the celestial sphere, created by projecting Earth's equator directly out into space.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

Every object exactly on the celestial equator has a Declination of 0°. The celestial equator always intersects your local horizon at exactly due East and due West, no matter your latitude!

☀️

Ecliptic

The path of the Sun

The Ecliptic

The apparent path of the Sun through the sky over the course of a single year.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

It is tilted at 23.5° relative to the celestial equator due to Earth's axial tilt.

The constellations intersecting this plane form the Zodiac. Eclipses only occur when the Moon's orbit crosses the Ecliptic (at points called nodes).

⬇️

Nadir

The point directly below you

Nadir

The point on the celestial sphere exactly opposite the zenith, directly beneath an observer.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

In the altitude-azimuth system, the Nadir has an altitude of exactly -90°. Zenith and Nadir are always separated by 180°.

📐

Altitude

How high an object is

Altitude

The angle of an object above or below the horizon, measured upward from 0° at the horizon.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

Horizon = 0°, Zenith = +90°. Objects with negative altitude are below the horizon and not visible in an ideal sky.

🧭

Azimuth

Compass direction on the horizon

Azimuth

The direction of an object measured around the horizon, usually starting at north and increasing eastward.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

In the common convention, North = 0°, East = 90°, South = 180°, and West = 270°.

🌍

Declination

Sky latitude

Declination

The angular distance of an object north or south of the celestial equator.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

Declination works like Earth's latitude: the celestial equator is 0°, the north celestial pole is +90°, and the south celestial pole is -90°.

⏱️

Right Ascension

Sky longitude

Right Ascension

The celestial coordinate that measures position eastward along the celestial equator from the vernal equinox.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

Right Ascension is usually measured in hours, not degrees. A full circle is 24h, so 1 hour of RA equals 15°.

🌞

Equinoxes & Solstices

Yearly milestones on the ecliptic

Equinoxes & Solstices

Equinoxes happen when the Sun crosses the celestial equator. Solstices happen when the Sun reaches its greatest north or south declination.

🏅 Olympiad Fact

On the equinoxes, day and night are nearly equal. At the solstices, the Sun is near +23.5° or -23.5° declination.
